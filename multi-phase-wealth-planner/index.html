What the multi-phase wealth planner does

Most calculators look at one stage of life. This planner joins three of them into a single picture: the years you spend building wealth, a stretch where the money simply compounds, and the years you draw an income from it. That makes it a useful tool for thinking about retirement rather than just accumulation.

You set a starting lump sum and a monthly SIP for the accumulation phase, a number of hold years where nothing is added or withdrawn, and then a monthly Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) with an optional annual escalation. The planner tracks your balance through every year and shows whether the corpus lasts.

How the three phases interact

In the accumulation phase your SIP instalments and starting capital compound together. In the hold phase there are no contributions or withdrawals, so the full balance compounds undisturbed — this is often the quiet workhorse of the plan. In the withdrawal phase the corpus keeps earning returns while you take out a monthly income that can rise each year to keep pace with rising costs.

Each month: Balance = Balance × (1 + i) + SIP − SWP

The sign of that monthly change decides everything. While the SIP is running the balance climbs; once the SWP starts, the balance only survives if returns keep pace with what you withdraw.

How to read your results

Watch the peak corpus reached at the end of the hold phase, the total SWP paid across retirement, and the terminal balance left at the end. If the planner flags depletion, the fix is usually to lower the SWP, reduce the escalation, or lengthen the accumulation or hold phases. To size the pieces, the SIP calculator and lump sum calculator are good companions.

Frequently asked questions

What does the Multi-Phase Wealth Planner model?

It models your money across three stages: an accumulation phase where you invest a monthly SIP, a hold phase where the corpus simply compounds, and a withdrawal phase where you draw a monthly income through a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP).

What is an SWP?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan lets you withdraw a fixed amount from your mutual fund holdings at regular intervals, typically monthly. It is commonly used to create a retirement income while the remaining corpus stays invested and continues to grow.

Why does the planner sometimes warn about depletion?

If your monthly withdrawals plus their yearly escalation grow faster than the portfolio earns, the balance shrinks and can reach zero before the withdrawal phase ends. The warning helps you adjust the SWP amount, the escalation, or the length of the earlier phases.

What is the benefit of the hold phase?

During the hold phase you neither invest nor withdraw, so the entire corpus compounds undisturbed. Even a few extra years here can significantly raise the income your SWP can later sustain.
