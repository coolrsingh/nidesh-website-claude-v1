What this lump sum calculator does

This lump sum calculator shows how a single one-time investment in mutual funds can grow when left to compound. You provide the amount, an expected annual return, and a time horizon, and the tool projects the value at the end of each year along with the returns earned on top of your principal.

Lump sum investing suits situations where you already hold a surplus — a bonus, a maturing deposit, or the proceeds of a sale — and you have the patience to stay invested through market ups and downs. The longer the horizon, the more the final value tends to outstrip the original amount.

How the compounding formula works

A lump sum grows by earning returns on both the original amount and on previously earned returns. The future value is given by the familiar compound interest formula:

Future Value = P × (1 + r)n

Here P is the amount you invest, r is the annual return as a decimal, and n is the number of years. Because the exponent grows each year, the value curve gets steeper the longer you stay invested — most of the growth in a long horizon happens in the final years.

How to read your results

The terminal value is what your money becomes; the returns earned is the difference between that and your principal. A modest change in the assumed return can have an outsized effect over many years, so it is wise to model with a realistic rate rather than a hopeful one.

If investing a large amount in one go feels uncomfortable, a Systematic Transfer Plan can move it into equity gradually. You can also compare a steady monthly approach with the SIP calculator, or plan an income drawdown later using the multi-phase wealth planner.

Frequently asked questions

What is a lump sum investment?

A lump sum investment is a single one-time amount placed in a mutual fund, as opposed to spreading it across monthly instalments. This calculator grows that amount at your chosen CAGR for the selected number of years.

Lump sum or SIP — which is better?

It depends on the money you have and the market. A lump sum can work well when you already hold a surplus and your horizon is long. If you would rather average your entry price over time, a SIP or a Systematic Transfer Plan from a liquid fund can reduce timing risk.

What does CAGR mean?

CAGR is the Compound Annual Growth Rate — the smoothed yearly rate at which your investment would have grown to the final value. It is a useful single number for comparing scenarios, though real returns are rarely identical every year.

Can I reduce the risk of investing a large amount at once?

Yes. Many investors park a lump sum in a low-risk liquid fund and use a Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) to move a fixed amount into equity each month, which averages the purchase cost over several months.
