What this loan eligibility calculator does

Before shortlisting a home, it helps to know what banks are likely to lend you. This calculator estimates that ceiling from your net monthly salary, the lending rate, the tenure you want and a FOIR cap. It returns the maximum EMI your income can support and the loan amount that EMI translates into.

The result is an indicative figure, not a sanction. Lenders also weigh your credit history, existing obligations, job stability and the property itself. Still, it gives you a realistic starting range so you can plan with confidence.

How eligibility is estimated

Banks cap the portion of your income that can go toward EMIs — this is the FOIR. The calculator first finds your maximum allowable EMI, then works backwards from that EMI to the loan amount using the present-value form of the EMI formula:

Max EMI = Salary × FOIR%  →  Max Loan = EMI × ((1 + i)n − 1) / (i × (1 + i)n)

Here i is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly instalments. A longer tenure or a lower rate raises the loan a given EMI can carry, which is why both levers matter when you are stretching for a property.

How to read your results

If the maximum eligible loan falls short of your target, you can add a co-applicant, clear existing EMIs, extend the tenure, or work on your credit score for a better rate. Once you know your range, use the home loan EMI calculator to see the monthly cost, and keep building wealth in parallel with a disciplined SIP.

Frequently asked questions

How do banks decide my loan eligibility?

Lenders look mainly at your repayment capacity. A common measure is FOIR — the share of your monthly income that goes toward all EMIs. This calculator applies your chosen FOIR cap to your salary to estimate the maximum EMI, then converts that into a loan amount.

What is FOIR?

FOIR stands for Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio. If your FOIR cap is 50% and you earn ₹1,00,000 a month, lenders may allow up to ₹50,000 of EMIs. Existing EMIs reduce the room available for a new loan.

How can I increase my eligibility?

You can add a co-applicant's income, close or consolidate existing EMIs, choose a longer tenure to lower the monthly EMI, or improve your credit score to negotiate a better rate. Each of these can raise the loan you qualify for.

Does my credit score affect the loan amount?

Indirectly, yes. A strong score (typically 750+) helps you secure a lower interest rate, and a lower rate means a given EMI can support a larger loan. It also improves your chances of approval.
