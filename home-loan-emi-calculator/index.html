What this home loan EMI calculator does

This EMI calculator turns three inputs — loan amount, interest rate and tenure — into the numbers that actually matter when you borrow: your monthly EMI, the total interest you will pay over the life of the loan, and the full amount repayable. It also shows a year-by-year schedule so you can see your outstanding balance fall.

Seeing the total interest in rupees is often a wake-up call. On a long home loan, the interest can rival or exceed the amount borrowed, which is exactly why the rate and tenure you choose are worth getting right.

How the EMI formula works

An EMI is a level monthly payment that covers both interest and principal on a reducing balance. It is calculated as:

EMI = P × i × (1 + i)n / ((1 + i)n − 1)

Here P is the loan amount, i is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by twelve) and n is the number of monthly instalments. Early in the loan, most of each EMI is interest; as the balance falls, more of every payment goes toward the principal.

How to read your results

Compare the monthly EMI against a comfortable share of your income, and look hard at the total interest. A small reduction in rate, a shorter tenure, or occasional prepayments can each save a large sum over the years. Before committing, it is worth checking how much you can borrow in the first place with the loan eligibility calculator, and planning your investments alongside the loan with the SIP calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How is the EMI calculated?

The EMI uses the standard reducing-balance formula. Each month, interest is charged on the outstanding balance and the rest of your EMI reduces the principal, so the interest portion falls and the principal portion rises over time.

What is the difference between fixed and floating rates?

A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period, giving predictable EMIs. A floating rate moves with the lender's benchmark, so your EMI or tenure can change when rates change. Floating rates are common for long home loans in India.

Does prepayment really help?

Yes. Because interest is charged on the outstanding balance, prepaying early reduces the principal on which future interest is calculated, which can shorten the tenure and cut total interest substantially.

Are there tax benefits on a home loan?

Home loan borrowers can typically claim deductions on principal repayment under Section 80C and on interest paid under Section 24, subject to the limits and conditions in force. A tax adviser can confirm what applies to you.
