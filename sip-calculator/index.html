What this SIP calculator does

This SIP calculator helps you picture how a regular monthly investment in mutual funds can grow into a meaningful corpus over time. You enter three things — the amount you can invest each month, the annual return you expect, and how many years you plan to stay invested — and the tool projects your final corpus along with how much of it is your own contribution and how much is growth.

The real lesson most people take away is the gap between what they put in and what they end up with. Over long periods, the returns component can dwarf the invested amount. That is compounding at work, and it is the single biggest reason to start a SIP early rather than waiting for a “perfect” time.

How the SIP formula works

A SIP is essentially a series of monthly investments, each of which grows for a different length of time. The calculator compounds every instalment from the month it is invested until the end of your tenure, which is the same as the standard future-value-of-a-series formula:

Corpus = P × [ ((1 + i)n − 1) / i ] × (1 + i)

Here P is your monthly amount, i is the monthly rate (your annual return divided by twelve), and n is the number of months. The early instalments compound the longest, which is why a SIP started a few years sooner can finish well ahead of one started later, even for the same total outlay.

How to read your results

Look at three figures together. Total invested is the discipline you bring; returns earned is what the market adds; and terminal corpus is the sum of both. If the corpus looks small, try extending the tenure before increasing the amount — time usually does more work than a bigger cheque. Once you are comfortable with a monthly figure, a small annual step-up keeps your investing in line with your income.

When you have a number that feels right, it helps to compare paths. You might also run a one-time lump sum scenario, or map your full retirement journey with the multi-phase wealth planner.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP and how does this calculator work?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. This calculator takes your monthly amount, an expected annual return (CAGR) and the number of years, then compounds each instalment month-on-month to estimate your final corpus, the total you invested and the returns earned.

Are the returns shown guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns are market-linked and vary year to year. The figure you enter is an assumption used only to illustrate the power of compounding. Actual outcomes can be higher or lower, which is why a realistic 10–14% is more sensible than an optimistic number.

What is a Step-Up SIP?

A Step-Up SIP increases your monthly instalment by a fixed percentage every year, usually in line with salary growth. Even a 10% annual step-up can meaningfully increase your final corpus, because the extra amount also gets the full benefit of compounding.

How long should I stay invested?

For equity mutual funds a horizon of at least five to seven years is generally suggested, and longer is better. Longer tenures smooth out market cycles and let compounding do the heavy lifting.
